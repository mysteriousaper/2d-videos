# WiyMood marketing playbook (read before every plan and every post)

Distilled from the research in `marketing/knowledge/research/` (Instagram, branding, marketing/leads, design/motion), 7 Nov 2026.
Phase 2 (Gemini watching the 109 videos in `watch-queue.tsv`) will confirm and extend it. House rules always win: Armenian first, «լոգո», never name clients, never invent numbers, no AI-generated people, every post approved in Telegram.

## 0. Sell, not just be present (team lead's direction, 13 Nov 2026)
Every piece of content and every agent run must move someone one step closer to a paid project. Being visible is not the goal; conversations, meetings and paid projects are.
1. **Every post has a job and a next step.** Each feed post is tagged as one of: *proof* (real work, demo, before/after), *problem* (an owner's daily loss that we fix), *offer* (a package or the free audit). Every caption ends with one concrete step: DM «ԲՐԵՆԴ», WhatsApp, or "send this to an owner who…". No post without a step.
2. **Mix per week (feed):** at least 2 *offer* posts, at least 2 *proof* posts (real work on screens and in real places, systems demos), the rest *problem* posts. Pure "inspiration" or entertainment posts only as stories.
3. **Sell outcomes in three doors, one team** (from the Transformation Blueprint): **Brand** (look as good as you are), **Digital** (get found and get orders: site, Maps, booking, ordering), **Systems** (run with less manual work: AI replies, CRM, dashboards). Lead with the door that matches the post's problem; never list everything we do in one post.
4. **Show what we really built.** Our own products (the ordering and admin system, the education platform) are shown as "built by WiyMood, available for your business", with real screens on real devices. Concepts are labelled "concept". Never present stock photos or mockups as client work.
5. **Natural before polished.** Real phone footage from the team (hands, desk, process, our work printed and placed in real places) beats templates; templates frame real content. No AI-generated people, faces or voices (house rule).
5a. **The account must not look automated (team lead, 13 Nov 2026).** Templates stay, but they are one of three layers, mixed every week:
   - **Templates (~40%)**: tips, checklists, offers, myth/fact, Wiy. Clean and useful.
   - **Philosophy / leader posts (~20%)**: the founder's own view in first person: how we think about brands, business, AI and craft; a short opinion, a lesson from a meeting, a value we work by. Simple typographic card or a real photo of the founder, signed with the founder's name. Text written with the founder (never invented quotes); one idea per post.
   - **Natural posts (~40%)**: real phone photos and clips from the team: process, desk, city, printed work in real places, our products on real devices, meetings (with consent). Light edit only, natural light, no heavy filters, small logo or none.
   Never 3 template posts in a row on the grid. Captions sound like a person (first person, plain words, an occasional real detail of the day), not like an ad. Vary layouts and posting times slightly; avoid identical cover styles for every post.
6. **Content feeds the pipeline.** Every DM, comment with «ԲՐԵՆԴ», story reply or WhatsApp becomes a prospect record the same day, gets a free audit offer, and is followed up (reply and followup skills). Outbound (prospect, outreach) runs in parallel: the team visits and calls; content gives them something to show.
7. **Measure money, not likes.** Weekly report leads with: new conversations, audits requested, meetings booked, proposals sent, deals won. Reach and likes come last.
8. **Prices stay private** (house rule): offers say what is included and the timeline, and "write us for the price"; numbers only in DMs, calls and proposals after someone asks.

### 0.1 Instagram that sells: lessons from 7 videos (13 Nov 2026, notes 228–234)
1. **One person, one problem.** Bio, highlights and the first 9 grid posts answer one owner's main problem, so a visitor who arrives from a reel sees more of the same and binges (228, 230, 232). Profile = billboard: who we help, the outcome, one step, contacts (232, 234).
2. **Founder-led.** People buy from people: the founder on camera and in first-person posts; real face, real voice, real opinions; a real headshot on every platform (234, 233). Weekly rhythm to adapt: story/authenticity post, authority post (problem solved + example), a "hand-raiser" post (we're taking 2–3 businesses for X), a second story, a second authority post, something light at the weekend (234).
3. **Depth beats basics.** Generic tips are answered by AI now; show real experience, real numbers we have, strong opinions that invite comments (229, 233). The truth sells, including what went wrong (233).
4. **Formula for ideas:** our topic + our opinion + a proven format from another niche, e.g. «5 բան, որ երբեք չէինք անի սրճարանի բրենդի հետ» ("X things I'd never do as a Y") (230).
5. **Test in sprints, score by money.** 30 reels in 30 days: ~10 adapted proven formats, ~10 remakes of our best, ~10 experiments; every 30 days score each reel by views, followers per 1,000 views and DMs/leads it brought; winners become next month's remakes (228, 229). Each reel is judged on its own: one flop means nothing (229).
6. **Shares and real comments matter most**; share rate ≥2% is strong (speaker's claim, 229). Ask questions that make owners tell their own story.
7. **Lead capture:** a keyword in the caption («ԲՐԵՆԴ») sends a genuinely valuable resource (a real audit, a checklist they'd pay for, never a 5-minute PDF) (228); a short form with a budget question before booking a 20-minute "fit call" (231); a story sequence 1–2× a week ending in one ask (228).
8. **Flagship entry offer:** a fixed-scope paid or free audit deck is easier to sell than a retainer (231); first projects become case studies and testimonials (231).
9. **Outbound with permission:** find local businesses via Google/Instagram search, ask permission first, then send a short personalised video or audit; no spam DMs (232, 234). Keep a "Dream 100" list of the businesses we most want (234).
10. **Production: quality volume.** Raw, honest phone video is fine; content beats polish; cut steps before automating (233).
11. **Relatable office meme (team reference, 13 Nov 2026):** real team footage, landscape clip letterboxed on black in a vertical frame, one white serif caption on top («Գրասենյակը, երբ…»), a trending sound, fast cuts between 2 angles, 15–30 s, no talking. Shows real people and humour; it humanises the page. Only the team and friends who agree to be filmed; never laugh at a client.

## 1. What makes Instagram show us to new people
1. **Sends beat everything for non-followers.** Every week at least 2 posts must be "send-worthy": something an owner forwards to a partner or friend ("send this to a café owner who…", an AI demo, a sharp before/after).
2. **The first 3 seconds decide a reel.** Hook text on frame 1 (it is also the cover and the muted hook). No slow intros, no logo opening.
3. **The first 24 hours decide a post** (Meta Q3 2026: ~45 % of recommended reels are <1 day old). Post at our slots (12:30, 19:30) and reply to every comment within the first hours.
4. **Originality is a ranking condition.** Our own designs, footage, voice. Stock photos ≤1 in 5 and never as the main subject; never reposts, never other apps' watermarks.
5. **Watch time and completion:** reels 15–45 s for growth, one idea, loopable ending. Carousels 6–10 slides, swipe-worthy (each slide earns the next).
6. **Saves come from value:** checklists, myth/fact, step-by-steps.
7. **Search:** Armenian + English keywords in captions ("բրենդինգ", "լոգո", "կայք", "branding"); never "Yerevan" (we serve businesses anywhere); max 5 hashtags, usually 2–3.
8. **Frequency:** at least 5 feed posts a week (2 a day maximum), plus stories most days.

## 2. What we say (strategy)
1. **Diagnose before prescribing (Ritson):** the free brand check is the diagnosis; posts show the problem first, the fix second, us last.
2. **Five recurring series = our category entry points**, each with the same title card:
   - «Բացում եք սրճարա՞ն» (opening a café/bakery)
   - «Մինչև զբոսաշրջային սեզոնը» (hotels, guesthouses, travel)
   - «Google Maps-ի էջը մեռա՞ծ է» (invisible on maps)
   - «Ամեն տեղ տարբեր լոգո» (inconsistent brand)
   - «Շատ DM, քիչ ամրագրում» (slow replies, AI assistant)
3. **Mix ~50/50:** brand-building (Wiy tips, process, before/after, small-business culture) and activation (offer, cases, «ԲՐԵՆԴ»).
4. **Core ideas to teach, one per post:** a brand is a gut feeling, not a logo · the customer decides your brand · every touchpoint is the brand · the "only" test · sell the feeling not the feature · consistency builds trust · price signals position · logo principles and the 9 common logo mistakes.
5. **Voice:** warm, ready, creative, premium = casual-leaning but polished, more serious than funny, calm and sure. "You" more than "we", short sentences, one vivid image per post, a concrete next step. No slang, emoji storms, ALL CAPS, fake urgency or "best agency".
6. **Copy patterns:** PAS (problem, agitate, solution) · specific callout ("Hotel owners on Abovyan…") · before/after/bridge · hidden-cost question · AIDA micro-post. Always end with one clear step (DM «ԲՐԵՆԴ», or "send this to…").

## 3. The offer
- **Free "7-point Brand & Maps Check in 48 hours"**: Google Maps listing, logo consistency, Instagram grid, reviews, DM speed, website, booking path; owner sends «ԲՐԵՆԴ» + profile link; delivered as a designed PDF or short screen video with their own screenshots and one quick win for today; honest cap of 5 checks per week.
- The 5 diagnostic questions: who you are / what you do / why it matters · ideal customer and what brings them · competitors and why you're the only choice · 3 words you want vs 3 words customers use · do logo, colours, Instagram, sign and messages look like one business?
- Paid starter (price only in private conversations, never in posts; team rule 8 Nov 2026): logo cleanup + Google profile + one-page site with booking/WhatsApp + Instagram templates + review QR; one honest guarantee; never promise rankings or bookings; urgency only from real seasons or real slot limits.

## 4. How it must look (every render)
- **Reels/stories safe zone 1080×1920:** text and logos only inside y 270–1248 px and 65 px from the sides (more on the right). Stories: nothing key in the top 250 px / bottom 380 px.
- **Feed 1080×1350:** headline inside the central ~1012 px band (the profile grid shows 3:4).
- **Contrast:** text only in these pairs: ink/paper, paper/deep, white/blue, blue/paper. Red, white-on-red or ink-on-red only for big text (≥66 px, ≥52 px bold). Never red on blue, ink on blue, blue on deep.
- No text under ~44 px; one focal point; red-orange used once (the CTA or the key word); Wiy and the blue on every post; Wiy or a face looks toward the CTA.
- **Video:** captions always burned in (most people watch muted); subtitles ≤42 characters per line, ≤2 lines; hold each text ≥ characters ÷ 17 seconds; element animations 100–400 ms, ease-out, nothing linear; icons start on their spoken word; one idea per scene.
- Run the 15-point QA in `research/design-motion.md` before uploading.

## 7. Additions from Library 2 (text sources, 7 Nov 2026)
**Armenian copy**
- Formal/client copy keeps ՛ on imperatives; casual captions may drop it (Գրեք մեզ). Always ՞ on questions, « » quotes, no line starting with ։ or ».
- Warmth from a few well-placed folk-colloquial words or idioms, never slang or vulgarity.
- Latin brand names may stay Latin; all copy around them is Armenian (Advertising Law art. 5).
- Never use GHEA Grapalat or GHEA Mariam in brand work (they read as government paperwork).

**Market**
- Instagram reaches about 1.45M people in Armenia (55.5 % women); Facebook 1.65M. Don't post identical content everywhere; adapt per platform.
- Tourism rises from spring to September; prepare hospitality and travel content before the season.
- "Armenia, The Hidden Track" clusters (nature, adventure, culture, gastro) are a ready content frame for guesthouse and café clients.

**Instagram craft**
- First 2 caption lines: the hook plus a searchable Armenian keyword («լոգո», «կայք սրճարանի համար»).
- Alt text on every image or slide; subtitles and a keyword on screen; 3–5 hashtags.
- Reels bring reach, carousels bring engagement: plan both every week.
- Hooks: conflict → «բայց» → «դրա համար»; payoff first; works muted in the first 3 s.
- Carousels: curiosity cover, one step per slide, open with the result, end with a recap and CTA.
- Reply to comments within the first hour.

**Branding and delivery**
- Every client project gets one strategic idea in one sentence, built from what the business already owns (history, place, signature product), and every touchpoint answers it.
- Present identities live with steering questions, never by email and never "Do you like it?"; no free spec work; value pricing.
- Websites we deliver: one main action above the fold, text menu, a page per service with the city, NAP identical to Google Business Profile, Web Vitals passing (LCP ≤2.5 s, INP ≤200 ms, CLS ≤0.1).

**Outreach and AI selling**
- Problem first, a fact we observed, under 80 words, one low-pressure question, max 3 follow-ups that each add something new, no buzzwords.
- AI assistants answer only from the owner's approved texts and hand everything else to a person; pitch "assistant for your staff", promise no numbers.

**2D explainers:** record the voice first, then animate to it (it sets the timing).

## 8. From the video library (87 videos, 7 Nov)
- **Ranking signals (Instagram's own explanation):** watch time first, then likes and sends. Sends matter most for reaching non-followers; likes a bit more for followers. Both total watch time and completion rate count, with no fixed penalty for length, so a longer reel is fine if people stay. Judge posts by sends per reach and average watch time, not views. (098, 099, 209)
- **Carousels are our strongest format in 2026:** each swipe counts, and a carousel skipped on slide 1 is shown again from slide 2. Keep 2–3 carousels a week. (104)
- **"Sandwich" carousel:** hook slide → a short video slide → CTA slide. The API supports a video inside a carousel, so build these when team footage exists. (104)
- **Entertain first, then teach:** specific relatable humour, a twist at the end, timely topics, series linked as playlists. (105)
- **Search beyond Instagram:** clear keywords in captions and on-screen text; public posts can show up in Google and AI search. (102)
- **Distinctive, not different:** the same assets every time (Wiy, the blue, the red dot, the wave) so people recognise us without reading. Reach many business owners, not a tiny niche; repeat the message over time. (088, 090)
- **Don't chase ghost followers or follow-for-follow:** an untargeted audience lowers watch time for every post. (098)
- **Only in the app (team):** add trending music to carousels and photos (helps them reach the Reels feed); Trial reels to test hooks on non-followers. (099, 102)
- **Show the process, not just the result:** before/after and sped-up screen recordings of a logo or page being built prove the thinking behind the work and build trust. (035, 037)
- **Process posts include the messy part:** rough sketch → wireframe → final, with numbers, arrows and short notes on each step; the mess before the polish is what builds trust. (059, 062)
- **A "contrarian take" series:** one thing most businesses (or agencies) do that we disagree with, and what we do instead; said the same way every time, it makes WiyMood memorable. Pairing × consistency = association. (044)
- **Layout check on every render:** one focal point; loud and quiet parts (if everything is big and bold, nothing stands out); related items grouped, not spaced evenly; real size contrast; empty space left empty. (051)
- **Low reach is not a "shadowban":** Instagram says it doesn't shadowban; any restriction shows in Account Status. Low reach means the post didn't hold attention or get sent. Rework the hook or format and repurpose what worked before. (099, 209)

**Additions from trainer run 1 (text sources, 7 Nov 2026)**
- **Skip rate is the hook test:** Instagram's reels skip rate = share of views that leave within the first 3 seconds. A high skip rate means frame 1 failed: fix the first frame and hook text before anything else. (902)
- **Our scorecard per post:** skip rate (reels), average watch time, sends/shares per reach, reposts, saves. Compare each post with our own median; Instagram publishes no benchmarks. Views alone (which count repeats) are not success. (098, 902)
- **Trial reels test one thing on non-followers:** change one variable (hook line, first frame, length or a new series format), read the result after ~24 h against our earlier trials, and share to followers by hand for experiments (auto-share decides on the first 72 h of views). A trial can still reach some followers via DMs or audio pages. (901, 902)
- **Music on carousels:** music makes a carousel eligible for Reels surfaces but is not a ranking boost by itself; a weak carousel can get music added or swapped after publishing. As a business account, promotional posts use royalty-free tracks (Meta Sound Collection or our own bed), never "popular song" claims. (903)
- **Sound design for reels:** pick the music bed before final timing; design only 2–4 moments (hook hit, the «բայց» switch, the reveal, the CTA), not a whoosh on every cut; build hits from 2–3 short layers, pitch-neutral or in the music's key; check the mix once on a phone speaker. Reels must still make sense muted. (908)
- **AI production, our way:** separate steps/sub-agents (plan, write, render, review) instead of one big prompt; the agent reads the relevant files and writes a plan before making anything; brand rules and reference images live in files every agent reads (CLAUDE.md, this playbook, brand folders), never only in a chat; several hook/caption variants within fixed limits; a log of hook hypotheses and results that feeds next week's writing; a reviewer pass against the playbook before anything reaches Telegram. (904, 905, 208, 215)
- **Each surface ranks differently:** Stories by predicted taps, replies and reactions from people close to us; Feed by catching people up (activity, popularity, past interactions); Reels by watch-to-end, reshares and audio-page visits; Explore by likes, saves and shares from people with similar interests. Plan each format for its own signal. (097)
- **Instagram search:** the typed text is the #1 signal, matched against usernames, names, bios, captions, hashtags and places, then the searcher's activity, then engagement; it also reads spoken audio and transcripts, which keeps posts findable long after the first days. Keep our keywords («բրենդինգ», «լոգո», «կայք», branding, website) in the name field, bio, caption first lines and say them aloud in the voice-over. Hashtags only categorise now (they no longer bring reach and can't be followed): 2–3 relevant ones, never stuffing. (100, 209)
- **Ask for sends, not comments:** shares and likes move views most (a DM send and a story share count about the same); comments matter least for reel reach. CTAs: "send this to a business-owner friend" beats "comment below" (except the «ԲՐԵՆԴ» lead keyword, which is for leads, not reach). (103, 209)
- **Hook types that work (pick one per reel, first 3 s):** curiosity/open loop, short real story, challenge ("we rebrand a café in 60 s"), pain-point ("your logo is costing you customers"), comparison (this vs that), and a visual jolt (zoom, fast cut, unexpected image) before any words. Value without a hook isn't enough. (170, 103)
- **Judge trends, not single posts:** check Insights weekly, don't overreact to one post; the algorithm changes in small steps daily, and big swings usually follow the calendar (holidays, school terms, the season), so compare like with like before changing strategy; check Account Status if reach collapses; original content beats reposting others. (097, 209)
- **Seed the first comment ourselves:** right after posting, the team adds a short, specific question or extra tip as the first comment; it starts the thread and gives people something easy to answer. (218)
- **Make it repostable:** the four kinds of posts people repost are relatable (an owner's everyday problem), cause-driven (why we do this), entertaining (a skit, a twist) and unexpected (a surprising fact); aim each reel at one of them. (218)
- **Keywords in three places:** the topic words go in the caption, the on-screen text and the spoken script, because Instagram indexes all three for search and recommendations. (219, 102)
- **Low-effort formats count:** talking-head tips and behind-the-scenes B-roll with text and voice-over beat polished but rare posts; consistency and the human face matter more than production. (219, 217)
- **Stories that work:** text on every frame (most watch on mute); one strong slide reaches the most people, 2–3 slides (hook → build → CTA) suit an offer, more than ~5 only reaches the warmest followers; put the poll/question sticker on an early slide; a story that flopped can return later with a new first slide; save the evergreen ones to Highlights. (220, 221, 913)
- **Tease the ending:** say or show early what comes at the end ("the 4th one surprises most owners") so people stay to the last second; the hook works on screen text, picture and sound at once. (224)
- **Remix what works:** when a post beats our median, turn it into another format (reel → carousel → story poll) within two weeks instead of inventing a new topic. (225)
- **A little absurd beats perfectly polished:** surprise, a counterintuitive angle and laughter stop the scroll; turn everyday small-business annoyances (late opening, lost keys, the Monday rush) into playful 2D scenes with Wiy, and explain the *why* behind customer behaviour, not just a statistic. (157, 094)
- **Write about the reader's world, not ours:** the first few words decide whether a caption is read, so open with the point, never a warm-up. Nobody cares about our mission, values or office news; every post passes the "so what?" test. Pair each fact with a scene the owner recognises (not "we design menus" but "a guest reads your menu in the dark at 9 pm…"). (213, 214)
- **Text animation: calm and premium:** text enters with a steep ease-out (fast start, slow settle), never linear and never bouncy; every reveal carries one quiet secondary touch (blur-to-sharp, a line-drawn mask reveal, a tiny scale settle, or tracking that widens on a statement); thin strokes only if the letters stay readable; reuse saved presets so every reel feels like one brand. (210)
- **Sound in layers, used sparingly:** a quiet ambience bed under the scene, whooshes only on the big moves, a riser just before the reveal and a hit on it, small practical sounds (click, pop, pen) on key UI moments; voice always on top; final loudness check with no clipping. This refines the 2–4 designed moments rule above, not a sound on every cut. (908, 211)
- **Covers and metadata work as one:** the reel cover carries a 3–6 word curiosity line (it shows in search, the grid and Google); the same topic words appear in the cover, the hook, the caption and the 2–3 hashtags, so Instagram files the post correctly. Keep text on screen too, so the reel works without sound or in another language. (106, 209)
**Additions from trainer run 2 (text sources, 9 Nov 2026): a new account's first comments and DMs**
- **Reply to every comment in the first hours, then again at 24 h:** in Buffer's data (700K+ posts, each account against its own past) posts with replies had about 21 % more engagement, positive for about 63 % of profiles; a correlation, not a promise. Batch replies into two short windows after our 12:30 and 19:30 slots; each reply is short and ends with a follow-up question or a small tip, not just a thank-you. (914, 915)
- **Make commenting easy:** ask a specific question with a one-word or one-emoji answer on the last slide or in the caption (playbook 8's "ask for sends" stays the default; a question CTA is for posts meant to start conversations). (914, insights 2026-11-09)
- **Stories are the DM engine:** end most story sets with one interactive sticker (section 5), answer every reply by DM, and turn good questions into the next carousel or reel; test topics in stories before polishing them into posts. (913)
- **Consistency beats bursts:** in Buffer's 26-week data, accounts posting in 20+ weeks had far more engagement per post than sporadic ones; never skip a week (minimum 3 feed posts even in a busy week). Ignore generic "best time" tables; use our own Insights after a month. (915)
- **Reel covers for the 3:4 grid:** the profile grid shows the central 3:4 band of a 9:16 reel (y ≈ 240–1680 of 1920), so the cover line and Wiy sit there; one cover template per series so the grid reads as a set of series; a designed image beats a random frame. (917)
- **Account settings:** keep "Suggested content" / recommendations enabled for the account, and post when Insights shows our followers are most active; re-check the slots once we have a month of data. (106)

## 9. Client branding work (from the video library)
Applies to every client identity project. Section 3's diagnostic questions and section 7's "one strategic idea" still apply.

**Strategy (always before any visual)**
- **No logo, colour or font work before a signed-off strategy:** purpose (why), audience, positioning, values, personality and voice, in writing, approved by the owner. Skipping straight to visuals is the mistake the library repeats most; for cafés, restaurants and hotels the concept (cuisine or stay style, voice, look) comes before any design piece. (003, 004, 005, 010, 013, 015, 017, 019, 036, 046, 216)
- **Discovery is questionnaire first, then a session:** send the questions before the call so the owner has thought it through, read the answers for gaps and contradictions, and dig into those live. Cover what / how / why, who buys and why, the problem solved, the feeling customers should leave with, competitors and goals. (005, 012, 017, 021, 033)
- **Research with the 3Cs (company, customer, competitors):** collect competitors' names, logos, colours and claims; place them on two axes (e.g. everyday ↔ special, conventional ↔ bold) and look for the empty corner. (003, 005, 018, 021, 032)
- **Audience by values, not only age and location:** 1–3 personas with goals, frustrations and what they care about. Design for them, not for the owner's personal taste. (005, 018, 025, 032, 033, 071)
- **Positioning is one thing the business owns in customers' minds, in one sentence:** never "for everyone". Template: "for people who believe X and want Y, we promise Z". Every claim on the site needs a proof point (a policy, a guarantee, a process, a real review). (007, 009, 021, 022, 069, 085)
- **Distinctive beats different for small brands:** they win by being instantly recognisable (the same name, colour, mark and pattern everywhere) and easy to find, not by inventing more "unique" features. (088, 089, 090)
- **Story and voice come from real material:** origin story, what the business is against, and flaws turned into features ("small kitchen" → "everything made by hand"). Don't sanitise it into corporate copy. (007, 029, 043, 095)
- **Voice is built from the audience, written down, and adapted by context:** 3–4 traits as "is / is not" pairs (warm but not cute), words to use and avoid, and how the tone shifts between posts, customer replies and announcements. Never base it on the founder's quirks; strip jargon. (025, 026, 027, 028)

**Identity design process**
- **Fixed order with two sign-offs:** brief (name, audience, 3–4 brand keywords, clichés to avoid) → research → 2–3 moodboard/stylescape directions → **client picks a direction** → many rough sketches → refine digitally → present in mockups → **approval** → guidelines and files. No execution before the direction is approved. (005, 012, 014, 015, 017, 019, 033, 034, 036)
- **Few concepts, well argued:** the direction is chosen at the moodboard stage, so present one strong logo concept with its reasoning (up to three only if the direction is still open), not a buffet. (017, 033, 034, 046)

**Logo rules**
- **A logo is a memory hook, not a description:** feeling over literal pictures (no coffee cup for a café, no bed for a guesthouse); fits the category but stands apart from it; one idea, not every service; not trendy. (032, 033, 035, 048)
- **Craft checks before showing anything:** works in black and white first; built on a grid or simple geometry; optically corrected (even stroke weights, open counters, thinner strokes on dark backgrounds); based on a good typeface modified where needed; reviewed after a break; tested tiny (favicon, avatar) and in a real mockup (sign, packaging, app icon). (016, 033, 034, 035, 037)
- **Shape language matches personality:** rounded forms feel friendly and approachable, squares and straight edges feel stable and trustworthy, angles and slants feel dynamic. (032, 058)
- **Deliver a responsive suite:** primary lockup, secondary (stacked/horizontal), mark or monogram, favicon, with clear space defined in units of the mark itself ("X"). (014, 019, 034, 040, 042)

**Colour and typography**
- **Colour system:** one dominant colour, 1–2 accents, and neutrals; tints and shades from the same hue (change saturation/lightness only); meaning comes from context and audience, not a colour-psychology chart; every text/background pair passes a contrast check; for websites also define success, warning and error colours. (010, 016, 052, 053, 054, 055)
- **Exact codes, never guessed:** HEX/RGB for screens, CMYK and Pantone for print via a conversion tool; don't flip files between RGB and CMYK carelessly (colours go muddy). (020, 048, 054)
- **Type system:** one display/heading font plus one readable body font (or a single super-family for hierarchy); never two fonts that look alike; no playful faces for formal brands; never more than three fonts in one design; left-align body text (justified text leaves rivers); kern and track logos and headlines by hand. (010, 014, 016, 046, 047, 056, 057, 058)
- **Fonts must be licensed and support the scripts the client needs** (Armenian included, OpenType): never "personal use only" fonts on client work. (056, 057)

**Guidelines and file delivery**
- **Size guidelines to the client:** a small business gets a one-page brand sheet; larger clients get a full book plus a one-page cheatsheet. Contents: strategy summary and story, voice, logo versions and clear space, misuse examples, colour codes, type hierarchy, imagery style, patterns/icons, real touchpoint examples. Each part shows spec → correct use → don'ts. (010, 020, 038, 039, 040, 041, 042)
- **Map the client's touchpoints first** (sign, menu, Instagram, website, packaging, cards) and design the key ones before writing strict rules. (038)
- **Handoff package:** organised folders (Logos / Mark / Patterns & icons / Social / Guide PDF); SVG + PNG for digital, PDF/EPS for print, each on light, dark and colour backgrounds; text outlined; guide links to the cloud folder; PDF compressed for email; no licensed font files, link where to get them; final source files after final payment (inference). (020, 033, 040, 041)

**Presenting, feedback and rebrands**
- **Present as a story, not a reveal:** goal and strategy recap → design principles the work follows → the work in realistic mockups next to the target customer → the reason behind each colour, shape and font. Feedback then becomes "does it fit the strategy?" instead of taste. (013, 017, 032, 034, 046, 055)
- **Handle feedback calmly:** never take it personally; ask for examples of what they mean; tie every change back to the audience and strategy; revision rounds are set in the contract. (015, 018, 033, 034)
- **Plan the case study at kickoff:** capture the "before" (screenshots, current logo in use, Maps listing), agree 1–2 goals we can check later, and ask for a short testimonial at handoff with written approval for any public use. (910)
- **Protect scope with a "parking lot":** new ideas raised mid-project go on a list and become phase 2 once the agreed outcome is delivered. (909)
- **Rebrands protect what customers already love:** people resist change, so audit metrics (repeat customers, conversion) and run discovery and 3Cs research first, keep the equity worth keeping, and fix positioning across every touchpoint, not just a website facelift. (021)
- **Brand copy about the customer, not the company:** mission and values statements are for the team's alignment; public touchpoints (site, menu, posts) talk about what the guest gets. (214, 213)
- **Simple beats clever:** brand strategy and copy a child could understand; humour and warmth are easy wins; never copy-paste a big brand's playbook onto a small business without adapting it. (091)
- **Start from the outcome:** before designing, write down what the business wants (more bookings, higher prices, a new audience), what it must be known for, and the proof it already has (its "credibility bank": problems solved, results, lessons). The brand and the posts are built on that proof, not on adjectives. (044)

## 10. Websites and landing pages (client work and our own site)
- **Triage before redesigning:** low clicks to the page = an ad/post problem, not a page problem; visitors leave without scrolling = the hero fails; long visits but no enquiries = content not skimmable or no clear benefit; wrong kind of leads = targeting/message mismatch. (086)
- **Fix in this order:** (1) hero clarity → (2) skimmability and relevance → (3) form friction. Never polish step 3 while step 1 is broken. (086)
- **The hero answers "what's in it for me?" in seconds:** benefit headline, one supporting line, one CTA, a risk reducer (free, no obligation, reply time), and a real photo of the product or place, all above the fold on a phone. (083, 085, 086)
- **Message match:** the page's headline repeats the promise of the post, ad or DM that sent the visitor. Mismatch is the biggest conversion killer. (085, 086)
- **One page, one job:** a single primary action, repeated at logical points; remove competing links and offers. (081, 084, 085)
- **Page order that sells:** pain and the old way → the method → the offer → benefits (not features) → proof → "how it works" in 3–4 steps → risk reversal → FAQ near the CTA → CTA. Write the headline last. (081, 083, 085)
- **Proof must be specific and real:** real photos and named or counted reviews; no stock or AI people, no generic "100 % satisfaction" badges; concrete differentiators instead of "premium quality"; each feature paired with the situation where the visitor needs it, and shown rather than claimed. (063, 083, 085, 213)
- **Short, plain, skimmable:** landing pages around 425–475 words (Unbounce benchmark), read aloud at a simple reading level, clear hierarchy, related items grouped together, icons always with text labels. (047, 081, 084, 156)
- **Forms:** as few fields as possible; don't ask for a phone number if email or WhatsApp is enough; ask the easy question first (progressive disclosure). (071, 083, 086)
- **Mobile first:** most traffic is mobile; body text ≥16 px, full-width buttons, and every page checked on a real phone before launch. (081, 084, 085)
- **Speed and perceived speed:** compress every image, show content immediately (skeleton placeholders, not blank screens or spinners), give visible feedback on taps. Speed is the top mobile satisfaction factor, and a page that loads slowly after an ad click wastes the ad budget; keep the path from click to booking or checkout free of extra steps. Web Vitals targets are in section 7. (071, 156, 073)
- **Test like this:** "If we change X, we expect Y in Z because…", one variable at a time, 50/50 split, enough time for real data. Start close to the conversion (CTA wording); with low traffic, track micro-conversions (clicks, scroll depth). (058, 071, 084, 086)
- **Follow-up is part of the site:** a warm confirmation message, reminders for unfinished enquiries or carts, and a chat/AI assistant for questions without leaving the page (section 7's assistant rules apply). (071, 085)
- **AI assistants we build for clients:**
  - A workflow with routing (booking / prices / hours / complaint → a person), not a free-roaming agent; give it only the actions it needs, each documented with examples. Simple and predictable beats clever. (905)
  - Collect first from the owner: FAQ answers, prices, hours, booking rules, tone words, and what always goes to a human. Write 4–5 example conversations in the client's voice and language and put them in the prompt. (906)
  - Before launch and after every change, run a test sheet of real questions incl. off-topic and tricky ones; prices, hours and address must be 100 % right; the hand-off to staff is designed and tested. (906)
  - Check whether an off-the-shelf connector or workflow already covers the need; build custom only for what is specific (voice, booking rules, WhatsApp/Telegram/Instagram DMs). Anything that sends, posts or pays waits for the owner's approval. (907)
- **Choice order and context change decisions:** the order of options, the default and the wording around them shift bookings without new features; respect customers' status, certainty, autonomy, relatedness and fairness (SCARF) in every flow. (093)
- **Search and AI search basics:** descriptive text, page titles and meta descriptions so pages appear in Google and AI answers; the same name, logo and location data on the site, Google Business Profile and listings; easy navigation and a clear location (physical availability); mobile-ready before any ads run. (072, 089, 102)
- **Build order:** grid, global text/colour styles and components (buttons, nav, testimonial cards) first; prototype in Figma before development; bring the developer in early. (039, 063, 083, 153)
- **Sketch and map before pixels:** a timeboxed rough sketch, then the visitor's path from landing to booking/order (A → B) to find friction, then a low-fidelity wireframe, then the palette and style guide, and only then high fidelity. Body text left-aligned, never justified; everything inside frames on a grid. (059, 061, 062, 051)
- **Line height is set, not left on auto:** every text style (site, menu, print) gets an explicit line height so text never looks cramped or loose; resize logos and icons proportionally (scale), never stretched. Possible post: a menu before/after on line spacing. (060)
- **Content in a strong container:** the client's content is the star, the site is the frame; for histories or project stages, consider a custom timeline (circular, axis, "tree rings" = one ring per year) instead of a plain list. Explore freely at concept stage, then make it intuitive. (157)

## 11. Sales, offers and pricing

**Free check that makes it click (team lead, 15 Nov 2026, first real «ԲՐԵՆԴ» lead):** look at their actual posts before writing; open with one fact and its real numbers from their page (e.g. "this edit: 14 likes, 13 shares, with 2 followers"), say *why* it works, give one growth step (a series, a format, a rhythm), keep generic tips last, 4 short paragraphs max, deliver within 24 h, no calls in the activity phase. Full rules and approved texts: `marketing/replies/library.md` (C0–C3).
- **Sell the outcome, not the deliverable:** pitch how customers will feel about, recognise and choose the business, not "a logo" or "a website". Branding is reputation; a logo alone gets misused. (001, 002, 003, 004, 013, 029, 036, 038)
- **Name a friction point we observed in their customer journey** (dead Maps page, a booking form nobody completes, slow DM replies) before mentioning any service; section 7's outreach format applies. (009, 081, 086)
- **Lead channels in this order (Hormozi's core 4):** warm outreach (check in, don't sell) → content → cold outreach (research the person and their recent news first) → paid ads last, with a real budget. A tiny boost proves nothing. (078)
- **DMs: engage before pitching:** genuine replies to their stories and posts first, then a free useful insight, then the offer. Be interested rather than interesting; always a real person, never a fake persona. (024, 043, 077, 078)
- **Give before asking:** free audits and insights (our brand check) build goodwill that later converts. (043)
- **Answer the buyer's three questions:** why change anything, why now, why us; ask them on every discovery call before any talk of price, and price against the outcome they name. (030, 909)
- **Never send a proposal or price list as a first contact:** call or meet first; the proposal then fixes what was agreed. When a prospect compares on price, send the solution first, confirm they agree with it, then name price and timing. (911)
- **Fixed quotes, not hourly rates:** each option has one fixed price, labelled as a quote with what's included; hourly billing punishes skill, distorts how the work is done and gives the owner no certainty. (909, 094)
- **Talk budget early with a bracket:** once the problem is clear, say a wide range in round numbers out loud (from our real price list, never invented), starting at the top, pause and listen to the reaction before any written bid. If they say it's out of budget, ask what they planned to invest and offer a smaller scope instead of a discount; if we're not the fit, point them to a cheaper path anyway (goodwill comes back). People often can't say up front what they'd pay, so read reactions rather than asking cold. (212, 094)
- **Three options, never one price:** so the middle is the natural choice and the top shows the full value; no take-it-or-leave-it. (012, 077) Team decision (8 Nov 2026): options are **problem-led**, never generic Basic/Standard/Premium: findings with evidence → fixes from `config/bundles.json` → "fix the main problem / main + next / the whole system".
- **Price signals position:** never compete on cheapest; charge what the judgement is worth; be ready to say "this costs more and it's worth it"; keep a minimum project size. (007, 024, 034, 069, 087)
- **Proposals show value greater than price:** what's included, steps, timeline and one honest guarantee; scarcity only when real (our weekly slot limit). (081, 039)
- **Proposal order:** thanks and summary of the call → their problem in their words and what it costs to leave it → the solution and how their day changes → what we promise (only what we control) and what is an assumption, incl. possible difficulties → three options with fixed prices → the real reason the offer expires → one simple next step. Russian and Armenian proposals in plain infostyle: facts and specific actions, no empty adjectives or "solve it forever" promises. (909, 911)
- **Case studies sell the decision, not the effort:** a short overview (written last) → the challenge and goals → one insight → the work in mockups or motion with the reason for key choices → results tied to the goals. Anonymised (business type, never the name) and only real, approved results; without measured numbers, show the qualitative change and say so. Send the case closest to the prospect's business type. (910)
- **Selling AI services:** we sell set-up, local language and brand voice, testing and ongoing care, not "access to AI" (owners can switch that on themselves); pitch simple automations that answer routine questions and hand the rest to staff, with an approval step for anything that sends, posts or pays. Stay in marketing, messages and booking (not finance). No promised deflection rates or saved hours before the client's own test data. (904, 905, 906, 907)
- **Paperwork every time:** a 15–30 min kick-off call to align budget, timeline and expectations → written proposal with scope and process → contract with revision limits and ownership → a shared progress tracker for the client. (012, 015, 033)
- **Proof without naming clients:** anonymised before/afters and process stories (house rule), and show the "how" openly. (003, 004, 035, 037)
- **Explain simply:** if a non-designer can't repeat the offer back, simplify it; make sure the owner really understands the proposal instead of nodding. (072, 108)
- **Sell brand guidelines as an operating manual:** they save money and keep the brand intact whenever staff or freelancers make materials. (038, 040)
- **Keep clients long-term:** relationships over one-off sales; set up post-project check-ins and referral asks. (007, 066, 087)
- **Never:** promise sales spikes, rankings, stable reach or view counts; say "give me more budget and sales will jump"; sell quick fixes without a brand foundation; send generic or mass pitches; lead with feature lists; sell ads or follower growth without the service and retention behind them. (007, 068, 082, 088, 089, 104, 209, 216)

## 12. Marketing strategy (advice we give clients)
- **Mental + physical availability drive growth:** be remembered at the buying moment (distinctive assets, repeated) and easy to buy from (Maps, booking, delivery, clear location, short path to purchase). (088, 089, 090)
- **Specific message, broad reach:** be clear about who it's for and why (Godin), but show it to the whole category, not a tiny niche (Sharp). Growth comes mostly from new customers. (069, 070, 088, 090)
- **Skip complex loyalty programmes for small clients:** they mostly reward people who would buy anyway; simple review and referral prompts are enough. (071, 088)
- **Advertising is a long-term force:** weak in the short term, strong over time; advise clients not to disappear when budgets tighten. (088, 089)
- **Think in funnel stages:** awareness → consideration → conversion → loyalty, with tactics for each; nobody buys on first sight. Split audiences by temperature: cold (never met them) needs education, warm (engaged) needs proof, hot (asked, booked, added to cart) needs a direct offer. (007, 071, 083, 085, 073)
- **Clarify the model first:** one core offer, clearly defined, before promoting several things at once. (077)
- **A discount is not a demand strategy:** cutting price only moves along the same demand; to grow it, change what people prefer (brand, distinctiveness) and how many people reach the business. (109, 088, 090)
- **Ads bring the first visit; service brings the second (hospitality):** advise cafés, restaurants and hotels to keep guest contacts in a simple CRM, write down service standards so every shift delivers the same experience, and refresh menu, seasonal touches and marketing each season. (216, 071)
- **Post what customers care about:** prices, specials, the place, the people at work, how-tos, not mission statements or company milestones. (214, 213, 209)
- **Ads hygiene:** landing page ready, fast and mobile-friendly first; negative keywords set; currency and time zone right at setup (they can't be changed later); UTM tags on every link so each sale traces back to its ad; review regularly, stop the ads that spend without results and move budget to the winners; royalty-free music only (Meta swaps out copyrighted tracks); study competitors' long-running ads in the free Meta Ad Library for hooks; explain to the owner where the money goes. (072, 078, 073, 215)
- **Meta ad account safety (advise every client):** ads run from a Business Manager with a Facebook Page, never a personal ad account; business name, address, phone and site match reality exactly; two-factor login for everyone; few people with access and limited roles; one payment card; don't keep editing live campaigns; the ad's claims must match the landing page. (076)
- **Perception is cheaper than reality:** small changes to wording, interface or the wait experience often solve what an expensive operational change would. Ask "what's the smallest change that would make a big difference?" before proposing a big project; creativity applies to pricing and the offer too, not only to ads. (092, 093, 095, 156)
- **Effectiveness over efficiency:** cheap, efficient channels are not automatically effective; keep some distinctive, high-quality touchpoints. (093)
- **Automate the repetitive, keep people on judgement:** good first automations are repetitive tasks in tools that have APIs (ad and post variants, weekly reports, reply drafts); a person approves before anything goes out. (904, 907)
- **Judge by business results, not vanity metrics:** enquiries, bookings and repeat customers over impressions and likes. (022, 087, 104)
- **Comments and stories for clients:** reply to every comment in two short daily windows (Buffer's data links replies to higher engagement); Add Yours prompts for regulars («ձեր սիրելի անկյունը մեր սրճարանում»), Close Friends for loyal customers' previews, a countdown before seasonal launches, and highlights as a menu/FAQ. Brand kits include highlight covers, a story template with room for a sticker and one reel cover template per series. (913, 914, 917)
- **A cadence they can keep:** advise about 3 posts a week every week rather than a daily plan dropped after a month. (915)
- **Instagram search for clients:** align the client's keywords across handle, name field, bio, captions, hashtags and website metadata; that's what makes them findable when locals type what they need. (100)
- **Client profile as a mini website:** bio = who you are, who you serve, how, and what to do next (one CTA and one link); Highlights for services, reviews, FAQ and how to order; pin the 3 posts that answer "why you". (217)
- **Client posts get found locally:** for clients (not WiyMood) add a location tag to every post and story, descriptive alt text on image posts, and 2 niche + 2 local hashtags; collab posts with neighbouring businesses share audiences. (225)
